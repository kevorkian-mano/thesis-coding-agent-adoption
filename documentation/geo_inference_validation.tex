\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Geo-Inference Validation Runs}\\[6pt]
  {\small\color{gray} Each run takes 50 users whose country is known from their GitHub location field,
  strips that field, and checks whether bio / company / email / org / network signals alone
  recover the correct country.}
\end{center}

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent\textbf{The metric that matters is Precision, not raw accuracy.}
When the pipeline returns \texttt{not\_specified} (a \emph{missed} case), it is being
honest - there is genuinely nothing to infer from. Those users remain labelled
\texttt{not\_specified} in the data, which is already acknowledged as a limitation.
They do not pollute any geographic analysis.

The only real risk is a \textbf{wrong} prediction --- when the pipeline confidently
assigns the \emph{incorrect} country. The key metric is therefore:

\[
  \text{Precision} = \frac{\text{Correct predictions}}{\text{Total predictions made}} \qquad
  \text{Coverage} = \frac{\text{Total predictions made}}{\text{Total users}}
\]

\section{Combined Results — All 3 Runs}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Run} & \textbf{n} & \textbf{Correct} & \textbf{Coverage} & \textbf{Precision} & \textbf{Wrong rate} \\
\midrule
Run 1 (seed=42)  & 50 & 27 & 64.0\% & 84.4\% & 15.6\% \\
Run 2 (seed=123) & 49 & 28 & 61.2\% & 93.3\% &  6.7\% \\
Run 3 (seed=456) & 50 & 33 & 72.0\% & 91.7\% &  8.3\% \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total / Average} & \textbf{149} & \textbf{88} & \textbf{65.8\%} & \textbf{89.8\%} & \textbf{10.2\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{8pt}
\noindent\textbf{Interpretation:} Across 149 ground-truth users and 3 independent random samples,
the pipeline achieves a \textbf{precision of 89.8\%} --- meaning that when it commits to a country
prediction, it is correct approximately 9 times out of 10. Coverage sits at \textbf{65.8\%},
meaning roughly one third of users who lack a location field also lack any secondary signal strong
enough to infer from and are conservatively left as \texttt{not\_specified}. This is expected and
honest behaviour: the pipeline abstains rather than guesses.

The wrong rate of \textbf{10.2\% of predictions} represents the actual risk to geographic analysis.
The most common failure pattern is diaspora developers --- users whose network, email, or company
signals point to their country of origin rather than their current country of residence.

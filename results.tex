\section{RQ1 -- Scope of Adoption (Global)}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Overall Rate}

\textbf{3,039 / 4,500 repos = 67.5\%} multi-signal adoption (95\% CI: 66.2\%--68.9\%).

About two thirds of active JS/TS repositories created after February 2025 carry observable evidence of AI coding agent use. This figure should not be extrapolated to older repos -- it applies only to repos born during the agentic era.

\subsection{Temporal Trend}

Adoption grew from 34.8\% (Feb 2025) to 81.3\% (Apr 2026). The 67.5\% average understates the current rate because it averages early-2025 repos alongside more recent ones. Recent repos (mid-2026) are near saturation.

Sept 2026 dip to 63.2\% is likely incomplete data for very recently created repos.

\subsection{Born-Agentic Pattern}

1,011 / 3,039 adopting repos (33.3\%) added their first AI signal within 7 days of creation. These repos reflect developers who integrated AI tooling from the first commit. As a share of all repos: 22.5\% globally, 12.0\% Egyptian (lower due to lower overall adoption).

\section{RQ2 -- Signal Undercounting}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Undercounting Delta}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Corpus & Single \% & Multi \% & Delta & McNemar $p$ \\
\midrule
Global   & 48.1\% & 67.5\% & \textbf{$+$19.5 pp} & $4.4 \times 10^{-192}$ \\
Egyptian & 25.3\% & 41.0\% & \textbf{$+$15.7 pp} & n/a \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

McNemar test: $\chi^2 = 874.0$, $p = 4.4 \times 10^{-192}$. Discordant pairs: $b = 876$ (multi-only), $c = 0$ (single-only). Multi-signal is a strict superset of single-signal on this corpus -- every repo detected by single is also detected by multi, plus 876 more.

Nearly one in five adopting repos would be invisible to a single-signal approach.

\subsection{Statistical Tests}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fisher's exact:} $p = 1.31 \times 10^{-6}$, OR = 0.334. Egyptian repos have approximately one third the odds of showing AI adoption vs global.
  \item \textbf{Mann-Whitney U:} $U = 125{,}797$, $p = 1.15 \times 10^{-7}$, $r_{rb} = 0.326$ (medium effect). Median signal count: 0 (Egyptian) vs 1 (global).
\end{itemize}

\section{Corpus Version Comparison (Methodological Finding)}
\label{sec:corpusversion}
% ============================================================

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Metric & Version 1 (old) & Version 2 (corrected) \\
\midrule
Filter             & pushed $\geq$ 2025-01-01 & created $\geq$ 2025-02-24 \\
Size               & 136 repos & \textbf{83 repos} \\
Multi-signal       & 17.6\% (24/136) & \textbf{41.0\% (34/83)} \\
Single-signal      & 11.8\% (16/136) & 25.3\% (21/83) \\
Delta              & 5.9 pp & 15.7 pp \\
Gap vs global      & $-$49.9 pp & \textbf{$-$26.5 pp} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The jump from 17.6\% to 41.0\% is not real adoption growth. It is the removal of pre-AI-era repos that were created in 2018--2024 but simply pushed to recently. Those repos predate the tools and naturally show near-zero adoption.

The 5.9 pp delta in v1 (vs 15.7 pp in v2) is also distorted -- older repos generate fewer signals of any kind, compressing both single and multi numbers toward zero, so the gap between them also shrinks.

\textbf{Finding:} A researcher using last-push filtering would report a 50 pp gap between Egypt and the global average. The correct gap after creation-date filtering is 26.5 pp. This illustrates why creation date filtering is essential when comparing corpora, and why corpus construction decisions must be documented transparently.
